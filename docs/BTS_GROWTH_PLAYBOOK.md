# BTS Shorts growth playbook

## What changed in the publisher

- The scheduled workflow is BTS-only. It needs a recent, source-linked item and a valid script; if either is missing, it skips publication instead of recycling an old record claim.
- Scripts must use the supplied headline and excerpt, open on the story's specific detail, explain the payoff, and end on a question fans can answer. The narration no longer spends time on a generic subscribe slogan.
- BTS scenes use generated text cards. The description links the news source and includes the story question. The uploader does not have permission to post or pin comments with its current upload-only OAuth scope, so the workflow does not claim it pinned one.
- Titles and hashtags are specific and restrained. Tags and hashtag stuffing are not a growth strategy; the story and viewer response matter more.

## Editorial format to test

Build repeatable series so viewers know what the channel offers, while changing the actual story and visual treatment each time:

1. **The headline in context** — one current report, clearly attributed, with one detail that helps a fan understand why it matters.
2. **BTS timeline** — a dated event with a reliable source and a short explanation of what happened.
3. **One fan question** — a real, story-specific question at the end; invite thoughtful replies without baiting arguments.

Use an immediate, readable first frame with the key name or event, large high-contrast captions, and a spoken opening that names the story. Remove greetings, repeated intros, unrelated stock footage, and visual elements that compete with captions. Keep only facts supported by the cited source. For historical claims or records, use a primary source and link it in the description before publishing.

## Four-week learning loop

Keep the current daily schedule only if the source and script quality gates pass. If they do not, a missed upload is better than a stale or unsupported claim. Review each Short in YouTube Studio at 24 hours and 7 days and record:

| Metric | What it helps diagnose |
| --- | --- |
| Shown in feed and chose to view / stayed to watch | Whether the first frame and opening earn attention |
| Engaged views and average percentage viewed | Whether the story holds attention after the hook |
| Subscribers gained per 1,000 engaged views | Whether the Short attracts viewers who want more from this channel |
| Likes and substantive comments per 1,000 engaged views | Whether the story prompts a positive fan response |
| Traffic source | Whether viewers found it in the Shorts feed, search, or elsewhere |

Compare Shorts with similar topics and durations. Change one thing at a time (for example, first-frame wording, story format, or ending question) and keep a simple log with the title, source, format, publish time, and metrics. After several comparable uploads, repeat the formats that improve both retention and subscriber conversion. Do not judge a format from one upload or chase view count alone.

## Limits and next data needed

The `upload_result.json` artifact confirms upload metadata and the video URL; it does not contain views, retention, swipe behavior, or subscriber conversions. The linked Short could not be inspected in this review, and no YouTube Studio export was supplied. So this playbook improves the production controls and sets up a test; it cannot honestly identify a proven viral formula or promise subscriber growth yet. An Analytics export for the Short and channel would allow a specific diagnosis.

## YouTube guidance

- Recommendations account for viewer appeal, engagement, and satisfaction; the opening should deliver on the promise. [YouTube: recommendations and performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en)
- Shorts Analytics includes feed exposure, viewed versus swiped behavior, engaged views, and subscribers. [YouTube: understand Shorts analytics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en)
- YouTube warns that repetitive or mass-produced videos with little original value may be ineligible for monetization. Add original explanation and editorial judgment rather than publishing interchangeable cards. [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Uploading more often is not itself a guarantee of growth; use a schedule the channel can sustain with quality. [YouTube: search and discovery FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
